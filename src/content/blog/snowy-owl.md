The snowy owl (*Bubo scandiacus*) is a large, pale owl associated with the Arctic tundra. Its white plumage, yellow eyes and powerful build make it one of the most recognisable birds of northern regions.

## A bird of open country

Unlike many owls that favour forests, snowy owls often hunt in wide, treeless landscapes. They use low mounds, rocks and other raised points as lookouts, scanning the ground for movement. Their thick plumage and feathered feet help them cope with cold conditions.

## What snowy owls eat

Small mammals are an important part of the snowy owl's diet, particularly lemmings where they are abundant. The owl may also take other rodents and birds. Breeding success and movements can be strongly influenced by changes in prey populations.

## Seasonal movements

Some snowy owls remain in Arctic regions through much of the year, while others move south during winter. The scale and direction of these movements vary between years and are influenced by food availability, weather and breeding conditions.

## Respectful observation

Snowy owls can be vulnerable to disturbance, especially when resting or hunting in open areas. Birdwatchers should keep a generous distance, avoid approaching birds for photographs and follow local wildlife guidance.

The species reminds us that even seemingly remote ecosystems are dynamic and closely connected to wider environmental change.
