Every year, many bird species travel between breeding and non-breeding areas. Some journeys span continents, while others cover shorter distances between nearby habitats. Migration is shaped by food availability, weather, daylight and the need to find suitable places to raise young.

## Why do birds migrate?

For many species, migration is a way to make use of seasonal resources. Birds may move toward regions where food is abundant or where conditions are suitable for nesting. When seasons change, they return or move elsewhere.

Not every bird migrates. Some remain in the same area throughout the year, while others move only when local conditions become difficult.

## How do they find their way?

Birds use several sources of information to orient themselves. Research indicates that different species can use the position of the sun, stars, familiar landscape features and Earth's magnetic field. The importance of each cue varies between species and circumstances.

Young birds may inherit broad migratory directions, while experience can help individuals refine routes and recognise important stopover sites.

## Rest stops matter

Long-distance migrants often need places to pause, rest and feed. Wetlands, coastlines, grasslands and forests can all provide essential resources along a route.

When these habitats are lost or degraded, birds may have fewer safe opportunities to recover during demanding journeys.

## Watching migration responsibly

Seasonal movements can create wonderful birdwatching opportunities. Observe from a distance, avoid disturbing roosting or feeding flocks and follow local guidance around sensitive habitats.

Migration reminds us that birds connect distant ecosystems. Protecting the places they depend on requires cooperation across borders and seasons.
