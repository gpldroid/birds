Keeping a companion bird is a long-term responsibility. Birds are intelligent, social animals with needs that go well beyond food and a cage. Good care begins with understanding the species and planning for its physical and behavioural wellbeing.

## Choose a suitable environment

Provide the largest safe enclosure practical for the species, with enough room to move and stretch the wings. Perches of varied safe materials and diameters help encourage natural foot movement. Keep the enclosure away from kitchen fumes, smoke, drafts and sudden temperature changes.

Birds also need supervised time outside the enclosure in a bird-safe room. Close windows and doors, cover mirrors where needed, and remove access to toxic plants, hot surfaces and electrical hazards.

## Offer a balanced diet

Diet varies significantly between species. Many companion parrots need a carefully balanced diet with suitable formulated food, vegetables and other species-appropriate items. Seed-only diets are not suitable for many pet bird species.

Fresh water should always be available. Ask an avian veterinarian for advice tailored to the species, age and health of your bird.

## Enrichment and social contact

Provide opportunities to forage, explore, chew safe materials and interact. Rotate enrichment items thoughtfully and allow predictable periods of rest. A bird's social needs depend on its species and individual temperament.

## Preventive veterinary care

Find an avian veterinarian before a health problem appears. Birds can hide signs of illness, so changes in appetite, droppings, posture, breathing or activity deserve prompt professional attention.

Responsible care means learning continuously and choosing the welfare of the bird over convenience.
