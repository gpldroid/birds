Raptors are birds of prey that include eagles, hawks, falcons, kites and many owls. They occupy a wide range of habitats and use different hunting strategies, but many share adaptations that make them effective predators.

## Eyes made for spotting detail

Many raptors have excellent vision. Their eyes are adapted to detect movement and detail over distance, helping them locate potential prey from a perch or while flying.

The shape and placement of their eyes differ among groups, reflecting their varied ways of hunting.

## Talons and hooked bills

Strong feet and sharp talons help many raptors grasp and hold prey. A hooked bill is useful for tearing food into manageable pieces. These features vary with diet and hunting style.

## Different ways to hunt

Some species soar over open landscapes, using rising air currents to stay aloft with little effort. Others wait quietly on a perch before making a rapid pursuit. Falcons are known for fast, agile flight, while many owls rely on specialised hearing and quiet flight to hunt in low light.

## Why raptors need healthy habitats

Raptors depend on functioning food webs and safe nesting areas. Habitat loss, poisoning, collisions and persecution can threaten populations in different regions.

Protecting large trees, cliffs, wetlands and open spaces can benefit raptors and many other species that share their habitats.
