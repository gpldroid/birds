The peregrine falcon (*Falco peregrinus*) is a powerful bird of prey found on every continent except Antarctica. Recognised by its long, pointed wings, dark hood and bold facial markings, it is one of the most widespread raptor species on Earth.

## Built for speed

Peregrines are famous for their hunting stoop: a steep, controlled dive in which the falcon folds its wings close to its body and accelerates towards airborne prey. The exact speed varies with conditions and measurement method, but the stoop makes the peregrine one of the fastest animals known.

Its pointed wings, compact body and strong flight muscles help it manoeuvre at speed. A specialised respiratory system also supports the high oxygen demands of active flight.

## Habitat and range

Peregrine falcons occupy an extraordinary range of environments, from coastal cliffs and mountain landscapes to wetlands and large cities. They often nest on ledges that provide a clear view of the surrounding area. In some urban regions, tall buildings and bridges offer suitable nesting sites.

## Diet and hunting

The species feeds mainly on birds, which it catches in flight. Its prey can include pigeons, shorebirds and smaller songbirds, depending on local availability. Peregrines usually hunt alone and use surprise, speed and positioning rather than prolonged pursuit.

## Conservation

Peregrine populations were severely affected in parts of the world by persistent pesticides during the twentieth century. Restrictions on harmful chemicals, legal protection and recovery programmes helped many populations rebound. Local threats remain, so conservation assessments should always be checked against current regional sources.

Observe wild falcons from a respectful distance and avoid disturbing active nest sites.
